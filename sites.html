Field sites

Where we sample

Four sites in Campania and Lazio (Italy), shaped by volcanic and hydrothermal activity: three methane-rich environments and one site where methane occurs only in traces. They were chosen for their contrasting methane levels, their rich microbial communities and their proximity to our laboratories, so that samples can be analysed shortly after collection.

Campi Flegrei caldera

Lago Grande (Astroni)

The largest of three small lakes inside the crater of the Astroni volcano, in the Campi Flegrei volcanic district near Naples. Fed by groundwater, it has an oxygen-free bottom layer rich in dissolved methane of biological origin and CO₂, partly of hydrothermal origin.

Campi Flegrei caldera

Lake Averno

A meromictic volcanic lake: its deep waters never mix with the surface layer, creating a permanently oxygen-free bottom. Methanogenic Archaea thrive in the anoxic bottom waters and sediments, making the lake a natural analog for subsurface environments on other planets.

Southern Apennines

Bolle della Malvizza

The largest complex of mud volcanoes in the southern Apennines. Here bubbling mud pools release gas from deep hydrocarbon-rich brines, offering a very different methane source from the volcanic lakes.

Colli Albani volcanic district · trace methane

Solforata di Pomezia

A natural degassing area south-west of Rome, in the Colli Albani volcanic district. Its emissions are dominated by CO₂ with hydrogen sulphide, while methane is present only in traces.

In the field

What we collect

Water

Water column

Samples pumped from selected depths, with in-situ measurements of temperature, pH, conductivity and redox potential.

Gas

Dissolved and emitted gases

Methane and CO₂ concentrations and their carbon isotopes, used to tell biological from geological methane.

Sediment

Lake and mud sediments

Cores and grab samples for mineralogy, geochemistry and DNA sequencing (16S rRNA and metagenomics).
